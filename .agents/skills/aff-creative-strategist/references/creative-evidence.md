# Creative evidence, interpretation and experiments

## What existing guidance supports

- [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/TikTok_CreativeCodes_May2023.pdf) teaches hook → body → close, early value, and suspense/surprise/emotion. TikTok reports **+16% watch time for suspense** and **1.7× view-through rate for surprise** from a cited 2021 US small-business ad study; the public summary does not expose enough method detail here to treat those as causal effects. They are *advertising-context signals*, not a promise of extra organic views or Shopee orders.
- [TikTok creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices) recommends a hook within the first six seconds, a value proposition early, visible people, sound, vertical framing and ongoing testing. It explicitly calls these starting points that vary by campaign.
- [TikTok video advertising guide](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/guides/video-advertising-guide) suggests showing use, asking a relatable question or revealing a credible surprise in the first seconds, and revising hooks when early drop-off is high. These examples inform hypothesis generation, not fixed scripts.
- [Shopee affiliate video guidance](https://help.shopee.vn/portal/10/article/141537) prioritizes product relevance and visible product use. A TikTok hook that delays or obscures the product may underperform or conflict with Shopee's guidance.
- [A 2026 Douyin observational study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/communication/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2026.1863794/full) found that most text/verbal hook features did not remain robust after adjustment, and its held-out prediction was near zero. It studied cultural heritage videos, not affiliate commerce, and did not analyze visual/opening-scene hooks. Its useful lesson is to test proposed mechanisms locally rather than treating a hook formula as a law.

## Five hook mechanisms to test, not templates to copy

1. **Result first:** show an authentic outcome, then reveal how it happened. Requires real result footage.
2. **Problem in context:** show the exact friction before introducing the item. Avoid a generic rhetorical question.
3. **Comparison:** compare two real options or before/after setups under stated conditions. Never fabricate the inferior side.
4. **Micro-demonstration:** start in the middle of an observable action; explain after the viewer sees it.
5. **Expectation reversal:** challenge a specific assumption and substantiate the reversal on screen; do not use misleading bait.

Example for a cable organizer, assuming actual footage exists: first frame shows one hand pulling a cable cleanly from a crowded desk; text says “Dây này không còn rơi sau bàn.” The next shots show attachment, a real pull, and removal. An alternative variant begins with the cable falling behind the desk. Both must use the same body if the experiment is **hook only**. “Ai tin nổi” or a dramatic fake accident may earn attention but does not explain or prove the purchase value.

## Experiment discipline

Register the hypothesis and primary metric before posting. Define the same observation window for variants. For paid distribution, use the platform's A/B experiment when available; random assignment gives a stronger causal estimate. For organic posts, distribution changes with audience, time, account state and early engagement: rotate posting conditions, repeat across multiple blocks, report raw counts and treat measured lift as observational.

[TikTok Ads Manager split testing](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/split-testing-variables?lang=en) explicitly permits the opening 2–3 seconds as a creative variable and requires one selected variable per split test. Its current compatibility table lists **TikTok Shop as incompatible**. Do not describe this paid test facility as an available experiment for a TikTok Shop affiliate account without a separate verified route.

Calculate relative lift with a named denominator: if 3-second hold is 24% for A and 30% for B, B is **+6 percentage points**, or **+25% relative**. This says nothing by itself about orders. Report view/reach, early hold, completion, product clicks, confirmed orders, commission and cost separately when available. Never infer a sales gain from a watch-time gain.

Use these questions in review: Did the first frame communicate a product-relevant event? Does the video pay off the hook with visible evidence? Does the product appear soon enough for its platform? Is the creative understandable muted? What single change could explain the measured difference? What observation would prove the proposed explanation wrong?
